## Purpose

This catalogue identifies principal human, organization, service, and oversight actors and establishes the outer limit of their capabilities. It does not grant access: authorization remains deny by default and depends on authenticated identity, Workspace, Membership, Role, Permission, attributes, resource policy, assignment, state, time, and risk.

## Scope

The catalogue covers public, individual, competition, administrative, governance, safety, audit, security, operations, support, integration, and worker actors. Canonical role definitions are in the [Domain Glossary](domain-glossary.md); detailed experience boundaries are in [Platform Sides and Capabilities](platform-sides-and-capabilities.md).

## Capability interpretation

- “Permitted capability areas” describes the maximum design envelope, not an access grant.
- Organization, school, geographic, or national titles never establish authority without Membership, Permission, Administrative Scope, Workspace policy, and where relevant Competition Assignment.
- An actor may hold multiple roles, but the authorization engine evaluates each requested resource and action in context. Combining roles must not bypass separation-of-duties or conflict rules.
- Privileged actors use individual named accounts; shared privileged accounts are prohibited.
- Human actors cannot delegate their own Session or Credential to a worker or integration. Service actors use distinct workload identities.

## Conflict-of-interest foundation

Judges, Chief Judges, Appeal Reviewers, organizers, Coaches, Guardians, Teachers, Competitors, governance members, and other actors may have actual, potential, or perceived conflicts. QMDB-P0-B01 does not invent the final conflict policy. Later requirements and workflows must implement:

1. a conflict declaration tied to actor, relationship, scope, time, and evidence;
2. review by an authorized actor who is not improperly conflicted;
3. recusal that removes access and work assignment for the affected scope;
4. replacement with an eligible, independently assigned actor;
5. exceptional authorization with documented basis, approvals, limitations, and disclosure policy; and
6. durable Audit Events and version history for declaration, review, recusal, replacement, exception, and closure.

Undeclared or unresolved conflict must fail safely according to the future approved policy. It must never be resolved through a silent assignment change or by deleting prior evidence.
